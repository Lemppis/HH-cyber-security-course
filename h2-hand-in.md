## a) Podcast

I listened and summarized an episode of Herrasmieshakkerit[^1]. The main talking points are listed below:

  * Conversation about the security of mobile operating systems (Android vs. iOS). They believe that iOS, and by extension iPhones, are still a bit safer since Apple has more control over the whole pipeline. However, Google has improved a lot over the years regarding update support and privacy policy etc. They raise as an example Apple’s EIM (Memory Integrity Enforcement) that would be basically impossible to integrate into the Android ecosystem.
    
  * They highlighted a news article from the US, where the president has signed a national security memorandum that allows some private companies to perform legal cyber reconnaissance attacks on foreign criminal organizations. The hosts feel like this is a potential security risk in on itself and usually these kinds of attacks are only allowed to be performed by the government itself.
    
  * Talk about overall security landscape of newly uncovered vulnerabilities. Time to exploit has gone down significantly in the recent years. This means companies need to release updates much faster. For example, Apple has had to expedite the release of new iOS update because of this phenomenon. This is largely due to the advances in AI, which makes it much more financially viable.
    
  * General discussion about AI models, and by extension agents, and their capabilities. For example, the incident at OpenAI where the agents infiltrated another company’s database after first escaping their own confinements. Also, the ability for AI to solve "impossible" mathematical problems etc. All of this seems to stem from AI’s inherent need to complete given tasks no matter what the cost.
    
  * The hosts find it very concerning how AI models are constantly being improved and how different AI-companies don’t really seem to be willing to stop the advancements, even when they themselves openly admit that the agents keep getting "out" and cause chaos internally and externally. It seems that most are missing the bigger picture and how this technology will impact our overall security now and in the future.
    
  * Hosts ask their guest what is actually meant by cyber-physical security. Huotari condenses the matter to the fact that nowadays basically all security solutions are connected to the internet. This makes all physical safety features inherently susceptible to cyber-attacks and need to be secured accordingly. He sees that this affects the overall attack surface area significantly – it is more trivial to gain access to physical locations through the use of internet (e.g., access management).
    
  * The guest talks about his company Louhe, which specializes in security solutions, especially access control in physical locations. These include how security solutions should be managed and what kind of factors need to be taken into account. They especially try to build intelligence into their solutions through the use data analytics, sensory data and audit trails, for example.

  * *Own thinking*: It has always worried me that everything from your fridge to your car comes with an internet connection. Is that just how things go (developments keep developing)? No one stopped to think that this might be a bad thing from a security perspective? Or is the security usually just an afterthought? It is something that is now commercialized, since the cyber-physical risks are becoming the new norm.


## b) Blog

I read and summarized a blogpost from Traficom[^3]. The main talking points are listed below:

  * Stable Overall Situation: Finland's general cybersecurity status remained relatively stable during the first half of 2026, though continuous attention and stronger resources are required.

  * Rising riskis with Microsoft 365 services: M365 account takeovers and sophisticated phishing attacks remain a primary threat across both public and private sectors.

  * Advanced Attack Techniques: Attackers increasingly use Adversary-in-the-Middle (AiTM) techniques to bypass traditional multi-factor authentication (MFA) by hijacking session tokens.

  * The Dual Role of Artificial Intelligence:
     * Threats: Attackers leverage AI to automate reconnaissance, find system vulnerabilities, organise more convincing social engineering scams, etc.
     * Defense: AI is also a vital tool for defenders to detect anomalies, analyze logs, and spot vulnerabilities. Still, it requires human expertise and constant monitoring.
   
  * Overall, Traficom highlights the importance of cybersecurity related measures in organizations, and that citizens stay vigilant.

  * *Own thinking*: How can a normal person really safely operate in the current cyber-climate? I'm not sure if I'm more concerned for the elderly or for the upcoming generations.


## c) Science that

I think the Traficom blog post[^3] highlighted the current landscape of phishing attacks well. They are the norm and are relevant in many different attack types.
